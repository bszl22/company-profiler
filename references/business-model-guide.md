# Classifying how a company makes money

Read this when the revenue model is not stated outright. The goal is to answer three questions in plain language: who pays, what they pay for, and how the price is set. Then name the model. Always mark it as an inference if the company has not said it directly.

## Common models and the signals that point to them

| Model | What it looks like | Signals to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Subscription SaaS (per seat) | Recurring fee per user | Pricing page with per-user tiers, "Team" and "Enterprise" plans |
| Usage-based | Pay per API call, token, message, GB or transaction | Pricing calculator, developer docs with rate limits, "pay as you go" |
| Transaction / payments take rate | Percentage of money flowing through the platform | Fee schedule (for example 2.9% + fixed fee), payments or marketplace products |
| Marketplace | Commission from matching buyers and sellers | Two-sided product, "take rate", seller and buyer onboarding |
| Platform + services | Software plus paid implementation, consulting or managed service | "Solutions", "professional services", forward-deployed or solutions-engineer job postings, custom quotes |
| Enterprise license / contract | Large annual contracts, priced by negotiation | "Contact sales" only, no public pricing, security and compliance pages |
| Hardware or devices | Sells physical products, often with a subscription | Store page, shipping and warranty info |
| Advertising | Users free, advertisers pay | Ad products and advertiser pages |
| Freemium / product-led | Free tier that converts to paid | Free plan, self-serve sign-up, upgrade prompts |

Most companies combine two or three. Say which one drives most of the revenue if that is knowable, and which are secondary.

## What to say in the brief

Prefer a sentence like: "Customers are mid-market banks. They pay an annual platform subscription priced by the number of monitored accounts, plus a one-off implementation fee." Avoid: "B2B SaaS."

## Signals about who pays vs. who uses

The user and the buyer are often different (employees use it, the CFO or CIO buys). Mention the buyer when it is clear, since it shapes the sales motion and which teams the company hires.

## Being honest about inference

- If pricing is "contact sales", say pricing is not public and that the model is likely enterprise contracts, based on the evidence.
- Do not quote revenue or ARR unless the company or a credible outlet reported it. Third-party estimates (for example from aggregator sites) should be labelled as estimates with the source.
